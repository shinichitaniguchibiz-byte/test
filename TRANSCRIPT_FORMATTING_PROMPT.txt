Purpose

Convert a raw Japanese-English radio lesson transcript into a complete, highly readable plain-text lesson document suitable for English study. The finished document should read like a professionally edited NHK radio English lesson transcript.

Input

The input is a raw automatic speech-recognition transcript. It may contain missing punctuation, broken sentence boundaries, duplicated overlap at audio-chunk boundaries, incorrect capitalization, mixed Japanese and English, uncertain speaker changes, and occasional recognition errors.

Output format

Return only the finished lesson text.

The output must be plain text.

Do not use Markdown.
Do not use asterisks for emphasis.
Do not use hash symbols for headings.
Do not use bullet points.
Do not use numbered lists.
Do not use tables.
Do not use code fences.
Do not add commentary before or after the lesson.
Do not include a revision report.
Do not include timestamps, confidence values, processing logs, or technical notes.

Core preservation rules

Preserve the complete lesson content.
Do not summarize.
Do not shorten explanations.
Do not omit dialogue lines, example sentences, practice questions, model answers, pronunciation drills, or closing conversation.
Do not invent content that is missing from the transcript.
Do not complete an incomplete sentence from general knowledge.
Do not translate English into Japanese.
Do not translate Japanese into English.
Keep English utterances in English.
Keep Japanese explanations in Japanese.
Preserve intentional repetition of English sentences and pronunciation practice.
Remove a repeated line only when it is clearly an accidental duplicate created at an audio-chunk boundary.

Recognition correction rules

Correct punctuation, capitalization, spacing, and obvious sentence boundaries.
Correct an obvious recognition error only when the surrounding lesson content makes the correction highly certain.
Use standard English spelling for clearly recognized English words and expressions.
Use natural Japanese punctuation for Japanese explanations.
Do not rewrite the instructor's explanation into different wording.
Do not improve, simplify, or reinterpret the teaching content.
When a word or phrase cannot be determined safely, write （聞き取り不明） in that position rather than guessing.
Remove clearly hallucinated subtitle credits, transcription instructions, repeated system-like phrases, and other text that was not spoken in the lesson.

Document structure

Use the lesson's actual order.
Use the following section titles when the corresponding content exists:

Lesson number and lesson title
Dialogue
Dialogue Explanation
Grammar and Vocabulary
Essential Expressions
Practical Usage
Pronunciation Polish
Closing Conversation

Section titles must appear as ordinary plain-text lines. Leave one blank line before and after each section title.

Do not create an empty section.
Do not move content to a different section unless the audio sequence and wording clearly establish that section.

Paragraph construction

Join fragments that belong to the same sentence.
Join consecutive explanation fragments into coherent paragraphs based on meaning.
Create a new paragraph when the explanation changes topic, moves to a new example, begins a new question, or begins a new practice stage.
Do not place every short ASR fragment on a separate line.
Do not combine unrelated explanations into one long paragraph.
A Japanese explanatory paragraph should normally contain a complete idea and flow naturally from one sentence to the next.
An English explanatory passage should also be joined into complete, readable sentences.
Leave one blank line between meaningful paragraph units.

Dialogue formatting

For a dialogue, place each speaker turn on a separate line.
Use the actual character name when the character name is clearly established by the lesson.
When the name is not established safely, use A: and B:.
Do not guess a speaker's name.
Keep one speaker's continuous turn together even when the raw transcript splits it into several fragments.
Use this form:

A: First speaker's complete turn.
B: Second speaker's complete turn.

Leave one blank line before and after a complete dialogue block.
Do not place commas between separate speaker turns.
Do not merge two speakers into one paragraph.

English example formatting

Place a complete English example sentence on its own line when it is introduced, repeated for practice, translated, or explained as a teaching example.
Keep the exact number of intentional repetitions when the repetition can be determined from the transcript.
Keep short pronunciation units such as What if, baked him, and cake ourselves on separate lines when they are being drilled individually.
Join English words that were incorrectly separated by ASR.
Separate consecutive complete English sentences with correct punctuation and spaces.

Japanese explanation formatting

After an English example, place its Japanese meaning or explanation in the following paragraph or line according to the lesson flow.
Keep related grammatical explanation together as a continuous paragraph.
Use Japanese quotation marks or parentheses only when they improve ordinary readability.
Do not add decorative symbols.

Practical Usage formatting

Keep the situation description, Japanese task, hints, model answer, and explanation in their original sequence.
Place the complete model answer on its own line.
Keep repeated reading practice after the explanation.

Pronunciation Polish formatting

Preserve the instructor's English introduction.
Preserve each pronunciation unit and each intentional repetition.
Keep the practice order exactly as spoken.
Use short separate lines for individual sound units and complete-sentence practice.
Do not collapse the entire pronunciation section into one paragraph.

Closing conversation formatting

Format the closing exchange as speaker turns when the speakers can be distinguished.
Preserve Japanese closing remarks in Japanese.
Do not translate a Japanese closing remark into English.

Final validation

Before returning the result, verify all of the following:

The output is plain text only.
No Markdown emphasis or list syntax remains.
No technical logs or timestamps remain.
English remains English.
Japanese remains Japanese.
Intentional repetitions remain.
Accidental chunk-boundary duplication is removed.
Dialogue turns are separated by speaker.
Explanations are joined into meaningful paragraphs.
No missing content has been invented.
No lesson content has been summarized.
