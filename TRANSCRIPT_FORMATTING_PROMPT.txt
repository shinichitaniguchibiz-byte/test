Purpose

Convert a raw Japanese-English radio lesson transcript into a complete, highly readable plain-text lesson document suitable for study and later reuse.

Input

You will receive one raw transcript produced from the original audio. The transcript may contain missing punctuation, broken sentence boundaries, inaccurate capitalization, mixed Japanese and English, repeated practice sentences, unclear speaker boundaries, recognition errors, or short fragments.

Output format

Return plain text only.
Do not use Markdown.
Do not use asterisks, bullets, numbered lists, tables, code fences, heading marks, decorative symbols, or emphasis marks.
Do not add an introduction, explanation of your work, summary, notes to the user, or closing comment.
Do not place the final text inside quotation marks.

Core preservation rules

Preserve all substantive content from the source transcript.
Do not summarize, shorten, omit, simplify, or paraphrase the lesson.
Do not translate Japanese into English.
Do not translate English into Japanese.
Do not invent missing dialogue, explanations, examples, names, grammar points, or answers.
Do not silently replace uncertain material with likely content from general knowledge.
Keep repeated English sentences and pronunciation drills when the repetition is part of the lesson.
Remove only obvious speech-recognition artifacts, duplicated decoder loops, transcription instructions accidentally inserted into the transcript, and unrelated subtitle-credit hallucinations.
When a passage cannot be restored with reasonable confidence from the source, write [不明瞭] at that position.

Text correction rules

Correct punctuation, capitalization, spacing, and obvious sentence boundaries when the intended wording is clear from the transcript.
Correct an obvious recognition error only when the surrounding source makes the correction highly reliable.
Keep Japanese technical terminology, grammar terminology, personal names, lesson labels, and English examples as spoken.
Do not modernize, rewrite, improve, or reinterpret the teacher's explanation.
Keep the order of the original audio.

Document structure

Arrange the document in the following order when those parts are present in the source:

Lesson title and lesson number
Topic or learning objective
Key sentence
Opening explanation
Dialogue
Dialogue explanation
Grammar and Vocabulary
Essential Expressions
Pronunciation or reading practice within that section
Practical Usage
Model answer and explanation
Pronunciation Polish
Closing conversation

Write each section title on its own line.
Insert one blank line before and after each section title.
Do not create a section that is not supported by the source.
If a section title is spoken in Japanese but clearly corresponds to a standard English section title, use the standard English title.

Dialogue formatting

Treat dialogue as a sequence of speaker turns.
Put each speaker turn on a separate line.
Insert one blank line between speaker turns when this improves readability.
Use speaker names only when the source clearly identifies them.
When names are not reliable, use 話者A：, 話者B：, 話者C： in order of first appearance.
Do not assign a speaker label to narration or teacher explanation.
Keep each English speaker turn as a complete, naturally flowing utterance rather than splitting it into fragments.
Keep consecutive sentences by the same speaker in the same turn unless the source clearly indicates a new turn.

English example formatting

Keep an English example sentence on its own line when it is presented for study, repetition, translation, grammar explanation, or pronunciation practice.
Keep repeated examples as separate lines in the original order.
Join fragments that clearly form one English sentence.
Do not merge separate repetitions into one line.
Do not convert spoken English into katakana.
Do not replace an English sentence with its Japanese meaning.

Japanese explanation formatting

Join consecutive Japanese explanation fragments into coherent paragraphs based on meaning.
Do not place every short spoken phrase on a separate line.
Start a new paragraph when the explanation changes topic, moves to another example, introduces a new grammar point, gives a translation, begins an exercise, or changes from explanation to dialogue.
Keep each paragraph focused on one meaningful unit and preserve the teacher's original sequence.
Use normal Japanese punctuation and paragraph flow.

Japanese and English mixed passages

When a Japanese explanation contains an English word, phrase, or sentence, keep the English in Latin letters and connect it naturally to the Japanese sentence.
Use spaces around English expressions only where they improve readability.
Do not translate or transliterate the English expression.

Exercise formatting

Keep the problem statement, hints, model answer, explanation, and reading practice in their original order.
Place the complete model answer on its own line.
Keep repeated model answers and chunked pronunciation practice when they are part of the audio.

Final verification

Before returning the result, verify all of the following:

The output is plain text only.
No Markdown or decorative markup remains.
No substantive source content has been intentionally omitted.
No unsupported content has been invented.
Japanese remains Japanese and English remains English.
Dialogue turns are readable and consistently separated.
Japanese explanations are grouped into meaningful paragraphs.
English examples and repetitions are preserved.
The final document reads as one continuous, coherent NHK-style English lesson transcript.
