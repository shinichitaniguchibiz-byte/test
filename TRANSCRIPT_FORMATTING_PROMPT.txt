Purpose

Convert a raw Japanese-English radio lesson transcript into a complete, highly readable plain-text lesson document suitable for English study. The finished document must read like a professionally edited NHK radio English lesson text.

Input

The input is an automatic speech-recognition transcript. It may contain missing punctuation, broken sentence boundaries, duplicated overlap at audio boundaries, incorrect capitalization, mixed Japanese and English, uncertain speaker changes, and recognition errors.

Output

Return only the finished lesson text as plain text.

Do not use Markdown.
Do not use asterisks, hash symbols, bullets, numbered lists, tables, code fences, decorative marks, or AI-style emphasis.
Do not add an introduction, completion message, editorial note, or explanation of the work performed.

English-first principle

English is the primary lesson text.
Keep all English dialogue, key sentences, example sentences, model answers, pronunciation drills, and closing conversations in English.
Do not translate English dialogue into Japanese.
Do not replace English sentences with Japanese summaries.
Keep Japanese explanations in Japanese as supporting explanation paragraphs.
Do not translate Japanese explanations into English.
Use the original spoken language for each passage.

Document structure

Use the lesson title first, followed by the lesson theme and key sentence.
Use the following section titles when the corresponding content exists:

Dialogue
Dialogue Explanation
Grammar and Vocabulary
Essential Expressions
Practical Usage
Pronunciation Polish
Closing Conversation

Write each section title on its own line.
Use exactly one blank line before the content of a section.
Use exactly one blank line between major sections.
Do not insert repeated blank lines.
Do not place a blank line after every sentence.

Dialogue formatting

Format dialogue as consecutive speaker turns.
Write one complete speaker turn per line.
Place the speaker label at the beginning of the same line in square brackets.
Write every speaker label in English.
Use a reliable English name when the speaker is identified, for example:

[Theo] Dad's birthday is coming up next week.
[Jennifer] Yeah, but Mom already booked dinner, right?

When the speaker's name is uncertain, use:

[Speaker A]
[Speaker B]
[Speaker C]

For presenters or instructors, use an appropriate English label such as:

[Host]
[Instructor]
[David]
[Rosa]

Do not use Japanese labels such as 話者A or 講師.
Do not put a colon after the closing bracket.
Do not insert blank lines between consecutive speaker turns in the same dialogue.
Insert one blank line before a dialogue block and one blank line after the entire dialogue block.
Keep consecutive sentences by the same speaker on the same line when they form one turn.
Separate a new speaker onto a new line.

Dialogue and Japanese explanation

Keep the dialogue itself as English speaker lines.
Move spoken Japanese translations or explanations out of the speaker-labelled dialogue block and place them in the following Japanese explanation paragraph.
Do not interleave Japanese translation fragments inside an English speaker turn.
Do not invent a translation that is absent from the source.

Explanation formatting

Join Japanese explanation sentences into coherent paragraphs based on meaning.
Keep closely related explanation sentences in one continuous paragraph.
Start a new paragraph only when the subject, grammar point, example, or teaching step changes.
Do not place every Japanese sentence in a separate paragraph.
Use one blank line between meaningful explanation paragraphs.
Preserve English words and expressions embedded in Japanese explanations in English spelling.
Correct only clear punctuation, spacing, capitalization, and obvious speech-recognition errors supported by surrounding context.

English examples and practice

Place each independent English example sentence on its own line when it is being presented or practised as an example.
Preserve deliberate repetitions used for listening, pronunciation, shadowing, or oral practice.
Do not collapse repeated practice sentences into one occurrence.
Do not join several independent example sentences with commas.
Keep an English model answer as a complete sequence of sentences.

Pronunciation Polish

Keep the instructor's English directions in English.
Use English speaker labels when the section contains a conversation between instructors.
Keep short pronunciation units such as baked him and what if on separate lines when they are practised independently.
Preserve repeated pronunciation attempts.
Do not convert pronunciation practice into a summary.

Accuracy rules

Do not summarize or shorten the lesson.
Do not invent missing dialogue, explanations, examples, speaker names, or corrections.
Do not silently rewrite uncertain content into a plausible sentence.
When a passage cannot be resolved from context, retain the closest source wording and add [Unclear] immediately after the uncertain passage.
Remove only clear duplicated overlap caused by audio chunk boundaries.
Preserve intentional repetition.
Correct a recurring English sentence only when another clearly recognized occurrence in the same transcript establishes the wording.
Use the most reliable repeated occurrence as the canonical wording.

Final spacing check

Before returning the text, ensure that:

There are no multiple consecutive blank lines.
There is no blank line between consecutive turns of one dialogue.
There is one blank line between major sections.
Japanese explanations are grouped into meaningful paragraphs rather than one sentence per paragraph.
English dialogue and examples remain the primary lesson text.
All speaker labels are English words enclosed in square brackets.
The result contains plain text only.
